Step 2B · About 5 min to submit · By Oct 2

Submit three employee contacts

This is BUAD 304's field research piece. You provide the names and work emails of three people at the same organization: a focal employee, one of their coworkers, and their supervisor. You contact one person, and they ask the other two. After 2A, the submission form shows up in SONA as a study listing.

  • All three work 30+ hours a week for the same employer, and have agreed to take part.
  • Work emails only. Personal addresses cannot be used.

  • Can't find three contacts? Switch to the extended lab track: send just your focal employee's name and work email to mor.research@usc.edu, and complete 3.0 credits instead of 2.0. See Alternative pathways below.

Those three steps unlock everything, and none of them earn credits. SONA shows 0 credits after 2B, which confirms your submission went through. Complete Steps 1 and 2A by Friday, Sept 25 and Step 2B by Friday, Oct 2, and you are guaranteed enough study opportunities to earn full credit.

Step 2B: finding your contactsWho qualifies, where to look, and what happens after you submit

Want the full walkthrough? The Step 2B guide at bit.ly/sona-buad304-2b covers common scenarios, what your contacts will see, and a pre-submit checklist. Your contacts have their own site at bit.ly/mor-workplace.

Why three people

Organizational behavior research needs several views of the same workplace. Surveying an employee, a coworker, and a supervisor lets researchers ask questions like whether employees and supervisors see leadership the same way.

Who qualifies

  • Focal employee: the one person you contact directly
  • Coworker and supervisor: both work with the focal employee, though not necessarily with each other
  • All three work 30+ hours a week. Contractors and temps count if they meet that.
  • Same employer. Different locations or departments are fine.

Where to look

  • Parents, the most common source
  • Aunts, uncles, cousins, and family friends
  • Your own workplace, if you work full time
  • LinkedIn connections and past internship supervisors
  • Coaches, teammates' parents, and volunteer contacts

Not eligible

  • Part-time employees
  • Personal email addresses
  • People at different employers
  • Different franchise owners, which count as separate organizations

Before you submit, confirm

  • All three work at the same organization, 30+ hours a week
  • You have work emails for all three
  • The coworker and supervisor both work with the focal employee
  • All three have agreed to take part

Tips

  • Get agreement first, from all three, before you open the form in SONA.
  • Expect 3 to 5 attempts. Most students ask several people before finding a match.
  • Double-check every name and email. Typos are hard to correct after you submit.

After you submit

  • SONA posts 0 credits, which confirms the submission. You also get a confirmation email from mor.research@usc.edu listing your contacts. If anything is wrong, email that address right away.
  • The research team checks each submission and contacts you through your USC email if something needs fixing.
  • Surveys go out in late October. Each takes about 30 minutes and can be paused and resumed. Reminders are automatic, so you never need to follow up.
  • Your status does not depend on whether your contacts respond, though all three responses make the research most valuable.

Prepare your contacts. Ask your focal employee to tell the other two: "Watch for an email from mor.research@usc.edu in late October. It's a legitimate USC research study. Check your spam folder if you don't see it."

Outreach templatesAn email, a text, and a phone script you can adapt

Email

Subject: Request to Participate in USC Marshall Workplace Research
Dear [Name],

I'm enrolled in an organizational behavior course at the USC Marshall School of Business and am contributing to research on workplace dynamics. I'm hoping you can help. My deadline to submit names is October 2, 2026.

The research team needs survey responses from three people at the same organization: an employee, one of their coworkers, and their supervisor. These three perspectives allow researchers to study how the same workplace is perceived from different roles.

Here is what participation involves: a thirty-minute survey about workplace experiences (it can be paused and resumed), sent by email in late October 2026 from mor.research@usc.edu. It is completely voluntary and confidential, and responses are never shared with employers.

If you are willing to participate, could you ask one coworker and your immediate supervisor if they would also be willing? Once all three agree, I would just need each person's name and work email address so the research team can send the survey invitations directly.

I truly appreciate you considering this request.

Thank you,
[Your Name]

Text

  • You: Hi, would you help with a USC class project? It's a 30-min workplace survey in late October.
  • Them: Sure, what do I need to do?
  • You: Ask a coworker and your supervisor if they'd also do it. I just need names and work emails for all 3.
  • Them: When do you need it by?
  • You: October 2, 2026. Thank you.

Phone

  • Open: "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. Do you have a minute?"
  • Ask: "I'm taking a class at USC Marshall. Would you take a 30-minute workplace survey in late October?"
  • If yes: "Could you also ask a coworker and your supervisor? I just need names and work emails."
  • Close: "Everything is confidential. My deadline is October 2, 2026. Thanks."

If they ask

  • Why all three? The research compares how employees, coworkers, and supervisors see the same workplace, so all three views are needed.
  • How long? About 30 minutes each, and they can pause and resume anytime.
  • Is it private? Yes. It is voluntary and confidential, and employers never see individual responses.
  • Questions about the survey? Your contacts can write to mor.research@usc.edu directly.

How invitations workWhy they arrive when they do
  • Each invitation email has a unique link, and that link is the only way to sign up. You cannot sign up by browsing SONA.
  • With about 1,500 students in the program, no single study can take everyone at once, so invitations go out in waves.
  • The team rotates through all qualified students evenly, so there is no need to compete for spots.

See a study in SONA but no invitation?

Studies can appear in SONA before every wave has gone out. If you did not get an email with a link, you are not in the current wave. That is normal. Please do not try to sign up without one, since your turn will come.

What affects timing

  • Setup: when you finished Steps 1, 2A, and 2B
  • Study fit: your prescreening answers match what researchers need
  • Availability: how many studies are running that week
  • Track record: consistent attendance

These affect timing and order, not whether you can reach 2.0 credits. If you finish setup by the deadlines, you are guaranteed enough opportunities.

Rules and cancelingHow to take part, and what to do if plans change

While taking a study

  • Please do not discuss study content with others, since it can bias their responses
  • Answer thoughtfully and honestly
  • Find a quiet space and silence your phone for online studies
  • Read the instructions before you begin, and give yourself enough time
  • In group sessions you may leave once the researcher dismisses you

Canceling

Plans change, and canceling properly is always better than missing a session.

  • In person: cancel at least 6 hours ahead, by replying to your confirmation email or in SONA
  • Online: if you cannot finish within the 3-day window, reply to the invitation to cancel

Excused

  • Canceled 6 or more hours ahead
  • With a valid reason, such as illness or an emergency

Unexcused

  • No-show without notice
  • Late cancellation
  • Repeated missed commitments

Unexcused absences move you down the list for future invitations, and repeated no-shows significantly limit your opportunities. Expired invitation links cannot be reactivated. If something comes up, contact us as early as you can.

Alternative pathwaysUnder 18, no employee contacts, or unable to take part

Age requirement

You must be 18 by Oct 23, 2026 to take part in studies. If you will not be, contact us no later than Sept 25 to arrange an approved alternative.

Extended lab participation

Can't find three contacts? Get your focal employee's permission, then email their name and work email to mor.research@usc.edu. The Lab contacts their coworker and supervisor for you, and you complete 3.0 credits instead of 2.0, about 6–9 studies.

Written assignment

For documented circumstances that prevent any participation: three 3-page analytical papers on approved topics. Requires justification and instructor approval by Sept 25.

Contact mor.sona@marshall.usc.edu as early as you can if you think you need an alternative. Last-minute requests are hard to accommodate.

Why this matters for 304The reasoning behind the requirement

The methods behind the findings

The research on leadership, motivation, teams, and decision-making in BUAD 304 comes from studies like these. Here you see how they are designed from the inside.

Lab and field, together

Lab studies isolate cause and effect under controlled conditions. Field surveys, like the one your contacts take, check that findings hold in real workplaces.

Research that gets published

Your participation feeds peer-reviewed publications that shape how organizations understand their people.

Participation is voluntary, and you may withdraw from any study at any time. Responses stay confidential, and every study is approved by the USC Institutional Review Board.

Account and loginRegistering, activation, and getting in

Why do I need a separate account?

SONA is not connected to USC systems like Blackboard or myUSC. Registering links you to the program and turns on study notifications.

No activation email?

It comes from outside USC, so search your inbox and spam for sona-systems. If it has not arrived within a few minutes, email mor.sona@marshall.usc.edu.

Unable to log in?

Use Forgot Password at the bottom of the login page, and SONA emails you a reset link. Your User ID is your NetID only, the part before @usc.edu.

The site looks yellow.

That is the BUAD 307 (Marketing) SONA site, which is separate. BUAD 304 uses marshall-mor.sona-systems.com.

Technical issues?

Email us right away with the error message, the browser you used, and the steps that led to it.
